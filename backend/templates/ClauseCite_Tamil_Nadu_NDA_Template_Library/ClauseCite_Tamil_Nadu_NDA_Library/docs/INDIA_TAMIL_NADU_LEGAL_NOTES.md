# India and Tamil Nadu legal considerations (review note)

**Prepared for the ClauseCite Tamil Nadu NDA Template Library. Legal position must be rechecked on the date of drafting, signing, disclosure and enforcement. This is not legal advice.**

## 1. Contract formation and enforceability
- Review the Indian Contract Act, 1872, including sections 10–11 (valid agreements and capacity), 23 (lawful consideration/object), 27 (restraint of trade), 28 (restraint of legal proceedings), 73 (compensation for loss) and 74 (stipulated sums / reasonable compensation).
- Do not add a broad post-employment non-compete by default. Any non-solicitation, no-hire, exclusivity, standstill or similar restriction requires fact-specific advocate review.
- Do not assume that a stated penalty or liquidated sum is automatically recoverable. Draft and assess damages provisions under sections 73–74 and applicable case law.

## 2. Tamil Nadu stamp and execution
- The instrument must be classified under the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 as applicable in Tamil Nadu, including Tamil Nadu amendments and the current Schedule I / relevant article.
- **No stamp-duty rate is hardcoded in this library.** Duty may depend on the instrument's substance, structure, consideration, transaction context, execution and amendments in force. Confirm the current official schedule, notifications and rules at the time of execution; seek adjudication from the competent authority if classification or duty is uncertain.
- Do not represent a DOCX as duly stamped, registered, notarised or legally executed merely because it contains a signature block.
- Check whether any separate transaction document, annexure, power of attorney, IP assignment, data-processing instrument or other instrument has its own stamping / registration treatment.
- For electronic execution, confirm that the chosen signature method is recognised for the document and transaction, that evidence and audit trails are retained, and that stamping requirements are separately met.

## 3. Electronic records and signatures
- Consider the Information Technology Act, 2000, including sections 4 and 5 on legal recognition of electronic records and electronic signatures, subject to statutory exclusions and the applicable rules.
- The parties should agree on the permitted signing platform, authentication, signatory authority, time-stamps, audit trail, delivery and counterpart process. Obtain advice if the instrument falls within a statutory exclusion or requires a particular form.

## 4. Personal data and data-sharing
- A confidentiality agreement does not by itself establish a lawful basis for processing personal data or replace a data-processing agreement.
- The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 have phased commencement under G.S.R. 843(E) and G.S.R. 846(E), both dated 13 November 2025. As at 30 October 2026, the commencement notification schedules the main substantive provisions in sections 3–17 and related provisions for commencement 18 months after publication (13 May 2027); section 6(9) and section 27(1)(d) have a one-year commencement date (13 November 2026). Check the precise text, subsequent notifications, amendments and rules on the date of use. Do not describe all DPDP duties as already operative before their notified commencement.
- Also review the Information Technology Act, 2000, applicable rules and any sector-specific privacy, cybersecurity, confidentiality, localisation, retention or regulatory requirements that apply to the parties and dataset.
- A data-sharing template should identify the data, purpose, roles, instructions, disclosures, access, security controls, incident reporting, data-subject request assistance, retention, deletion, audit, sub-processing and cross-border transfer arrangements.

## 5. Remedies and dispute resolution
- Interim or permanent injunctive relief is discretionary and depends on the facts and applicable law; do not promise that an injunction will automatically issue.
- If arbitration is selected, comply with the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, including a written arbitration agreement, a clearly identified seat, appointment mechanism, language and number of arbitrators. Review arbitrability and interim-relief options.
- A Chennai / Tamil Nadu court clause must identify a court that independently has territorial and subject-matter jurisdiction. Contract wording cannot create jurisdiction where none exists.

## 6. Intellectual property and technology
- An NDA protects confidentiality; it does not itself transfer ownership of copyright, patents, inventions, software, designs or other IP.
- Review the Copyright Act, 1957 (including sections 17 and 19 for first ownership and mode of assignment), the Patents Act, 1970, applicable design / trademark laws, employee and contractor agreements, open-source licences and third-party rights as relevant.
- Use a separate signed IP assignment / licence / services agreement where rights are to be transferred or licensed. Tailor reverse-engineering restrictions to statutory exceptions and the transaction.

## 7. Template-specific advocate review
- Employee: employment status, protected disclosures, statutory rights, post-employment restrictions and employer policies.
- Consultant/vendor: subcontractors, access controls, data processing, security incident deadlines, IP ownership and service agreement precedence.
- Investor/M&A: securities, competition law, clean teams, standstill, exclusivity, no-contact and announcement restrictions.
- Customer/data sharing: privacy roles, data-processing terms, sector rules, cross-border transfer, security and retention.
- IP/technology: ownership chain, patent filing, copyright assignment, software interoperability, reverse engineering and open-source components.
